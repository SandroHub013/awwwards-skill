The argument

These four projects have nothing in common. That is the portfolio.

Eco packaging, a 1930s pizzeria, a parish church, and a bankruptcy-auction firm. Put them in a grid and they read as noise. Put them one at a time, each holding the whole screen on its own terms, and they read as range.

Every colour and every typeface named below is the one specified in the original case. Nothing on this page is a reproduction of the finished artwork — each project is set as a specimen, not a portfolio shot. Why, in the colophon.

01 / 04

Food packaging · identity and packaging system

BioBox

Eco-conscious food packaging, built on a cube-and-leaf mark: the box is the product and the mark is the box. Green appears once, against linen and charcoal, which is what keeps it from reading as supermarket “natural”.

Three faces, three jobs. A heavy display cut for the name, a text face for shelf copy, a neutral grotesk for the small print — the discipline that makes a packaging system survive contact with legal text.

02 / 04

Pizzeria · identity, mascot, signage

La Bussola

A 1930s register, taken seriously rather than as pastiche: a drawn mascot, a fat high-contrast display face, and red held against beige and black so the whole thing reads at signage distance.

The date is the brief. Pick the era, then commit to its type, its colour and its drawing — a retro identity fails the moment one element is contemporary.

03 / 04

Parish · institutional identity, liturgical print

San Carlo
Borromeo

An identity for a parish, with the liturgical materials that go with it. Red and gold are not a colour choice here — they are the calendar. The palette had to be inherited, then made to work in a booklet.

This is the only project on the page with two accents, and the only one where two accents are correct. Everywhere else it would be decoration. The gold set in type here is darker than the gold in the sample beside it: print gold is a metallic that catches the light, and on a screen the same value measured 1.96:1 against linen — below the 3:1 that large text has to hold.

04 / 04

Bankruptcy auctions · rebrand

Centro
Fallimenti

A rebrand for a sector nobody wants to look at, solved by refusing to be sombre about it: a geometric mark, acid green and orange on black, and a grotesk with enough width to hold a long compound name.

It is the one dark identity in the set, so it inverts the page — header, rail, focus ring and text selection all follow the tokens, because they all read the same six variables.

Colophon

What this is, and what it is not.

The work

The four identities are the work of Riccardo Petrini, published as Graphic Design Portfolio 2026 on 29 July 2026. Palettes, typeface specifications, sectors and tools on this page are taken from that case. See the original on Behance.

The substitutions

No mark, packaging render, illustration or photograph from the original is reproduced here, and the four specified type families are not licensed for this build. Names are set in the system stack instead; the identity shift is carried by the contrast between families, and the substitution is declared rather than disguised.

The build

One HTML file, one stylesheet, one script. No framework, no font requests, no raster assets — the largest element on the page is a text node. Made with the awwwards skill as a test of whether its method holds when the imagery budget is zero.
